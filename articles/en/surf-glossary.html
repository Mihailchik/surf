Surfers talk in their own slang. These are the words you will hear at a surf school, in the line-up and in a forecast, each with a short explanation.

Waves and the ocean

  • Swell. Waves that travelled from a distant storm. They are even and powerful. In Phuket the swell comes with the southwest monsoon, May to October.
  • Wind swell. Waves raised by local wind. Short, weak and uneven.
  • Period. Seconds between two waves. The longer it is, the stronger and cleaner a wave of the same height.
  • Set. A group of several waves in a row, usually the biggest. There is often a lull between sets.
  • Peak. Where the wave starts to break. You take off from there.
  • Shoulder. The unbroken part of the wave beside the peak. That is what you ride.
  • Lip. The crest that curls over and falls forward.
  • Barrel, tube. The hollow under the curling lip. Riding through it is every surfer's dream.
  • Close-out. The whole wave breaks at once along its length, so there is nowhere to ride.
  • Whitewater, soup. A wave that has already broken. Beginners learn to stand up on it.
  • Shorebreak. A wave that breaks right on the sand in shallow water. Dangerous to ride.
  • Beach break, reef break, point break. Where the wave breaks: over sand, over a reef or off a headland. Kata is a sandy beach break, Kalim near Patong is a reef.
  • Glassy. No wind, the water is smooth as glass. The best conditions to surf.
  • Offshore. Wind from the land out to sea. Holds the wave up and keeps it clean.
  • Onshore. Wind from the sea onto the beach. Breaks the wave early and chops the water.
  • Rip current. A narrow flow from the beach out to sea. It can take even a strong swimmer out. More in the guide on red flags.
  • Tide. The water level rises and falls twice a day, and a beach works differently at each stage. Our forecast shows the preferred tide for every beach.

Boards and gear

  • Softboard, soft top. A board with a soft deck. Surf schools use them: stable and they do not hurt when you fall.
  • Longboard. A long board, 9 ft (2.7 m) and up. Catches small waves and is easy to paddle.
  • Funboard, mini-mal. A mid-length board, 7–8 ft. Usually the next board after the learner one.
  • Shortboard. A short, manoeuvrable board up to 6–6.5 ft. Needs more powerful waves and experience.
  • Leash. A cord that ties the board to your ankle. Without it the board washes away after a fall and can hit someone.
  • Fins. The keels under the tail of the board. They keep it on course.
  • Wax. Rubbed onto the deck so your feet do not slip.
  • Nose and tail. The front and back ends of the board.
  • Rash guard, rashie. A tight top against sun and chafing. With water at 29–31° you do not need a wetsuit in Phuket, but you do want a rash guard.

Riding

  • Paddling. Moving on the board lying down, using your arms. Most of a surfer's time in the water is paddling.
  • Take-off. The moment the board catches the wave and the surfer stands up.
  • Pop-up. The move itself: from lying down to standing in one jump.
  • Regular and goofy. Your stance: left foot forward is regular, right foot forward is goofy.
  • Frontside and backside. Riding facing the wave or with your back to it.
  • Duck dive. Pushing the board under a wave and diving with it. Works on a short board.
  • Turtle roll. Rolling over under water with the board to let a wave pass. Used on a longboard.
  • Bottom turn. The turn at the bottom of the wave that every manoeuvre starts from.
  • Wipeout. Falling off a wave. It happens to everyone.
  • Washing machine. When a wave lands on you and spins you around under water.
  • Session. One go in the water, from paddling out to coming back to the beach.

The line-up and the rules

  • Line-up. The spot beyond the whitewater where surfers sit on their boards and wait for waves.
  • Priority. The wave belongs to whoever is closest to the peak and up first.
  • Drop-in. Taking off in front of a surfer who has priority. The worst thing you can do in the water.
  • Snaking. Paddling around someone to get closer to the peak and steal their wave.
  • Channel. A stretch with no breaking waves, often over a current. The easy way to paddle out.
  • Kook. A mocking word for someone who ignores the rules and gets in the way. Do not take other people's waves and do not let go of your board, and you will not be one.
  • Locals. People who surf the spot all the time. Respect them in the line-up.

Forecast words

  • Wave height. In a forecast this is the average height of the biggest third of the waves. Single waves in a set can be bigger.
  • Swell direction. Where the waves come from. A beach only works when the swell arrives from the side it is open to.
  • Window. The hours when wave, wind and tide line up. On the home page Surf shows today's window for every beach.
  • Flat. No waves at all, the sea is flat.
