May to October: the wave season

The southwest monsoon arrives in May and the picture changes within a couple of weeks: in April a wave of 1 metre or more happens on 2% of days, in May already on 49%. From June to September a day without waves is rare: 0.6 m or more on 96–99% of days.

July and September are the biggest months: a typical day brings about 1.3 m, and one day in ten more than 2 m. The waves are wind-driven, with a short period of 6–7 seconds, so the sea looks uneven and choppy.

For swimming this is the dangerous season. On days with waves, open beaches develop rip currents and lifeguards put up red flags. A red flag means stay out of the water, even where it is shallow.

November to April: calm sea

In November the waves fade fast: days with 1 metre or more drop to 7%. From December to April the sea is flat, the usual height in open water is 0.5–0.6 m, and on beaches inside bays that is close to no waves at all.

One curious detail: the swell period in the dry season is longer, 9–11 seconds. These are echoes of distant storms in the Indian Ocean. They rarely bring height, but now and then they deliver a small clean wave that works on a longboard.

What it means for surfing

  • May and October are the edges of the season. Waves do not come every day, but they are often small and friendly for learning.
  • June to September: there is almost always a wave. The question is not “is there one” but “is it too windy”: quality depends on the wind and the tide.
  • November to April: surfable days are rare.
